## 3. Channel Performance

### Evidence

Analysis 02 shows that channel performance differs across divisions, with Retail, Distributors, E-commerce, Direct-to-Consumer, and Specialty contributing differently depending on the division.

A channel-level validation shows that Retail is Aurevia's largest revenue channel, generating £65,542.37 of net revenue, or 35.82% of total company net revenue. Distributors contribute a further £44,300.12, representing 24.21% of total net revenue. Together, Retail and Distributors account for 60.03% of company net revenue.

The remaining revenue is distributed across E-commerce (14.76%), Specialty (13.01%), and Direct-to-Consumer (12.20%). Despite differences in revenue scale, aggregate gross margins are relatively close across channels, ranging from 62.17% to 63.59%.

### Interpretation

Channel performance is not uniform across Aurevia's divisions. A channel that performs strongly for one division may have materially different revenue and margin characteristics for another.

### Management implication

Channel strategy should distinguish between revenue scale and channel economics. Retail and Distributors are the two largest revenue channels, accounting for 60.03% combined, and therefore have a material influence on overall company performance.

Aggregate gross margins are relatively similar across channels, ranging from 62.17% to 63.59%. However, gross margin alone does not capture the full economics of each channel. Differences in customer acquisition, returns, fulfillment costs, discounting, customer retention, and working capital requirements may materially affect channel-level profitability.

Management should therefore evaluate channel opportunities using both revenue contribution and broader channel economics before allocating additional investment.

However, the current analysis reports country-level results rather than aggregated cluster or regional totals, so further analysis would be required before drawing conclusions about overall cluster or regional performance.

### Management implication

Management should evaluate geographic performance using both revenue contribution and margin rather than relying on country size alone.

## 7. Discount Performance
### Evidence
- Aurevia generated £191,888.18 of gross revenue and recorded £8,920.50 of discounts, resulting in £182,967.68 of net revenue.

- The enterprise discount rate is 4.65%, meaning Aurevia realized 95.35% of gross revenue after discounts.

- Enterprise gross profit was £114,244.98, corresponding to an overall gross margin of 62.44%.

- At the division level, Wellness and Personal Care have the highest discount rates at 4.96%, followed by Everyday Essentials at 4.35% and Home & Lifestyle at 4.29%.

- At the channel level, Distributors have the highest discount rate at 4.77%, followed by Retail at 4.69%, E-commerce at 4.64%, Specialty at 4.55%, and Direct-to-Consumer at 4.40%.

- Discounting varies across division × channel combinations. Personal Care × Specialty has the highest observed discount rate at 5.75%, followed by Personal Care × E-commerce at 5.65%.

- Higher discount rates are associated with lower revenue realization within the reported combinations because discount rate and revenue realization are calculated from the same gross-revenue base.

- Gross-margin performance does not follow the discount-rate ranking uniformly. For example, Personal Care × E-commerce has a 5.65% discount rate and a 59.99% gross margin, while Wellness × Direct-to-Consumer has a 5.61% discount rate and a 63.03% gross margin.

### Interpretation
Aurevia's overall discount exposure is moderate at 4.65% of gross revenue, but discounting varies across divisions, channels, and division × channel combinations.

The variation indicates that discounting is not distributed uniformly across the commercial portfolio. Certain combinations have materially higher discount rates than the enterprise average.

However, the current analysis does not establish that higher discounting causes lower gross margins or lower profitability. Gross-margin differences can also reflect product mix, product costs, customer mix, or other commercial factors.

The evidence therefore identifies areas where discounting warrants further investigation rather than establishing a causal relationship between discounts and financial performance.

### Management implication
Management should monitor discounting at the division × channel level rather than relying only on the enterprise-wide discount rate.

The highest-discount combinations should be investigated to understand whether the additional discounting is associated with sufficient commercial value, such as incremental volume, customer acquisition, retention, or strategic account development.

Particular attention should be given to Personal Care × Specialty and Personal Care × E-commerce because they have the highest observed discount rates in the current analysis.

Before changing discount policies, management should evaluate discounting alongside product mix, customer economics, volume, returns, and profitability. This would help distinguish commercially productive discounting from discounting that may reduce revenue realization without generating sufficient incremental value.

## 9. Returns
### Evidence
- Aurevia sold 13,504 units and recorded 237 returned units, resulting in an overall return rate of 1.76%.

- Total refund amount was £3,221.87. Estimated product cost associated with returned units was £1,217.20, producing an estimated gross-profit impact of £2,004.67, equivalent to approximately 1.10% of company net revenue.

- Product return rates vary across the portfolio. Multi-Surface Cleaner and Daily Vitamin Complex have the highest observed return rates at 2.67%, followed by Scented Home Candle at 2.40% and Nourishing Hand Cream at 2.39%.

- Scented Home Candle records the largest number of returned units at 36, followed by Hydrating Body Wash at 32 and Daily Vitamin Complex at 30.

- At division level, Home & Lifestyle has the highest return rate at 2.27%, followed by Wellness at 1.81%, Personal Care at 1.72%, and Everyday Essentials at 1.35%.

- Retail records the largest number of returned units at 87, reflecting its larger sales volume. Direct-to-Consumer has the highest channel return rate at 2.51%, followed by Specialty at 2.04%. E-commerce has the lowest observed channel return rate at 1.35%.

- Customer return rates vary materially across the customer base. Andes Consumer Supply has the highest observed return rate at 4.37%, followed by Aurevia Online Store Germany at 3.76% and Urban Health Collective at 3.23%.

- Europe has the highest regional return rate at 1.90%, compared with 1.63% in Emerging Markets and 1.56% in North America.

- At country level, Italy has the highest return rate at 3.00%, followed by Spain at 2.79% and Germany at 2.14%. The United Kingdom accounts for the largest number of returned units at 53.

- Return reasons are relatively distributed across four categories. Customer preference accounts for 27.55% of refund value, Product quality for 27.50%, Incorrect item for 23.71%, and Damaged product for 21.25%.

- The monthly return-rate series varies substantially over time rather than showing a consistent upward trend. Observed peaks include 4.88% in March 2023, 5.21% in April 2024, and 5.40% in February 2025.

### Interpretation
Aurevia's overall return rate is relatively contained at 1.76%, but the aggregate figure masks meaningful variation across products, divisions, channels, customers, and countries.

Return exposure is concentrated in specific parts of the portfolio. Home & Lifestyle has the highest division-level return rate, while Direct-to-Consumer has the highest channel-level return rate. At the country level, Italy and Spain show higher return rates than the overall company rate.

The return-reason distribution does not point to a single dominant cause. Customer preference and product quality together account for approximately 55% of refund value, while incorrect items and damaged products account for the remaining share. This suggests that return management may require different interventions depending on the underlying reason rather than a single company-wide response.

Monthly return rates show periodic spikes rather than a simple sustained deterioration. The observed peaks therefore warrant investigation into specific products, customers, channels, operational events, or sales cohorts associated with those periods.

The estimated gross-profit impact of £2,004.67 provides a useful measure of direct product-economic exposure, but it does not capture other potential costs associated with returns, such as reverse logistics, handling, inspection, replacement, customer service, or inventory effects.

### Management implication
Management should investigate return performance at the product × channel × customer × geography level rather than relying solely on the enterprise return rate.

Products and customer relationships with materially higher return rates should be examined to determine whether the underlying issue relates to product quality, customer expectations, order accuracy, fulfillment, or other operational factors.

The return-reason distribution also suggests that interventions should be differentiated. Product-quality and damaged-product returns may require operational or product actions, while customer-preference returns may require investigation of product positioning, customer expectations, or channel-specific selling practices.

The recurring monthly spikes should be investigated against commercial and operational events before any structural conclusion is made.

Finally, the estimated £2,004.67 gross-profit impact should be treated as a partial economic measure. A fuller return-cost analysis would incorporate reverse logistics, handling, replacement, and other operational costs before management assesses the total financial impact of returns.

## Cross-Analysis Findings
The individual analyses provide a consistent view of Aurevia's commercial and economic performance. The most decision-relevant insights emerge when revenue, gross profit, margins, discounting, concentration, geography, customer behavior, and returns are considered together.

### 1. Aurevia's recent revenue weakness has not been accompanied by a broad deterioration in gross margin
Enterprise net revenue declined from £47,770.47 in 2022 to £44,587.92 in 2024 before recovering modestly to £44,977.02 in 2025. Gross profit followed a similar pattern, while gross margin remained broadly stable at approximately 62.4%–62.5% throughout the period.

This indicates that the change in enterprise revenue scale was not accompanied by a material deterioration in aggregate gross-margin performance.

The evidence therefore shifts the management question away from simply asking whether Aurevia remains profitable and toward understanding which divisions, channels, products, customers, and markets explain the changes in revenue scale.

**Management insight:** The evidence does not indicate an enterprise-wide breakdown in gross-margin economics. Management should instead investigate where revenue has contracted or recovered and whether the areas generating growth are also generating attractive economic returns.

---

### 2. Gross-profit contribution is concentrated among a relatively small number of products and customers
Wellness generates the largest division-level gross profit at £40,782.88, while Retail generates the largest channel-level gross profit at £40,747.87.

At the product level, the five largest products by gross profit generate approximately £75,806.75, representing approximately 66.4% of total company gross profit. These products also generate approximately £121,142.65 of net revenue, representing approximately 66.2% of company net revenue.

Customer profitability is also concentrated. The five largest customers by gross profit generate approximately £51,623.21, representing approximately 45.2% of total company gross profit.

The evidence therefore shows that a relatively small number of products and customers account for a substantial share of Aurevia's economic contribution.

**Management insight:** Concentration creates both an opportunity and a dependency. Management should understand the resilience of major profit contributors, the operational exposure associated with them, and whether future growth would increase or reduce concentration.

---

### 3. Margin percentage and absolute gross-profit contribution provide different views of performance
Home & Lifestyle has the highest division-level gross margin at 63.81%, while Wellness generates the largest absolute gross profit at £40,782.88 with a 63.18% margin.

Similarly, Specialty has the highest channel-level gross margin at 63.59%, while Retail generates substantially more gross profit at £40,747.87 because of its larger revenue base.

This demonstrates the distinction between:

- margin percentage,

- revenue scale, and

- absolute gross-profit contribution.

A higher gross-margin percentage does not necessarily translate into greater absolute gross-profit contribution.

**Management insight:** Management should evaluate commercial opportunities using both margin quality and economic scale. Gross margin alone is insufficient for determining where additional attention or investment may create the greatest enterprise-level impact.

### 4. Discounting is relatively consistent at aggregate level, but varies meaningfully across division-channel combinations
Division-level discount rates range from 4.29% to 4.96%, while channel-level discount rates range from 4.40% to 4.77%.

Wellness and Personal Care have the highest division-level discount rates at 4.96%. At channel level, Distributors have the highest discount rate at 4.77%, followed by Retail at 4.69%.

Revenue realization also varies across channels, with Distributors at 95.23% and Retail at 95.31%. In this analysis, revenue realization is mechanically related to discounting because both are calculated from gross revenue and discount amounts.

The profitability evidence does not support treating discounting as the sole explanation for margin differences. For example, Wellness has a 4.96% discount rate while maintaining a 63.18% gross margin, whereas Everyday Essentials has a lower 4.35% discount rate but a 61.02% gross margin.

**Management insight:** Discounting should be evaluated in commercial context rather than treated as an isolated problem. Management should investigate whether the incremental revenue or strategic value associated with discounts is sufficient to justify the reduction in revenue realization and how that relationship varies by product, customer, and channel.

### 5. The channel portfolio combines significant revenue concentration with relatively similar gross margins
Retail and Distributors collectively account for approximately 60.0% of company net revenue, making them central to enterprise performance.

Retail generates the largest channel gross profit at £40,747.87, while Distributors generate £27,553.72. Their gross margins are relatively similar at 62.17% and 62.20%, respectively.

Return behavior differs across these channels. Retail has a 1.74% return rate, while Distributors have a lower 1.57% return rate. Direct-to-Consumer has a higher 2.51% return rate despite representing a smaller share of company revenue.

The evidence therefore indicates that channel performance cannot be fully assessed through revenue and gross margin alone.

**Management insight:** Channel evaluation should combine revenue scale, gross-profit contribution, discounting, returns, and other channel economics that require further analysis, such as fulfillment and working-capital requirements. These additional factors should be measured before major changes to channel investment are made.

### 10. Aurevia's central management challenge is balancing growth, economic quality, concentration, and operational exposure
Taken together, the evidence indicates that Aurevia's performance cannot be understood through a single KPI.

The company maintains a stable enterprise gross margin of approximately 62.4%, but revenue scale weakened over the historical period before a modest recovery in 2025.

At the same time:

- gross-profit contribution is concentrated among a relatively small number of products and customers;

- Retail and Distributors account for approximately 60% of net revenue;

- discount rates vary across division-channel combinations;

- several economically important products and customers exhibit elevated return rates;

- geographic markets show materially different operational outcomes; and

- higher gross-margin percentages do not necessarily correspond to the largest absolute gross-profit contributions.

These findings create a set of interconnected management trade-offs rather than a single enterprise-wide performance problem.

**Management insight:** The next strategic question is not simply where Aurevia can generate more revenue, but where it can pursue sustainable growth while maintaining attractive economics and managing concentration and operational exposure.

This requires management to evaluate the trade-offs associated with discounting, customer concentration, channel mix, geographic variation, and returns, while distinguishing areas that warrant protection from those that require further investigation.
