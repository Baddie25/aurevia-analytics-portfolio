## 3. Channel Performance

### Evidence

Analysis 02 shows that channel performance differs across divisions, with Retail, Distributors, E-commerce, Direct-to-Consumer, and Specialty contributing differently depending on the division.

A channel-level validation shows that Retail is Aurevia's largest revenue channel, generating £65,542.37 of net revenue, or 35.82% of total company net revenue. Distributors contribute a further £44,300.12, representing 24.21% of total net revenue. Together, Retail and Distributors account for 60.03% of company net revenue.

The remaining revenue is distributed across E-commerce (14.76%), Specialty (13.01%), and Direct-to-Consumer (12.20%). Despite differences in revenue scale, aggregate gross margins are relatively close across channels, ranging from 62.17% to 63.59%.

### Interpretation

Channel performance is not uniform across Aurevia's divisions. A channel that performs strongly for one division may have materially different revenue and margin characteristics for another.

### Management implication

Channel strategy should distinguish between revenue scale and channel economics. Retail and Distributors are the two largest revenue channels, accounting for 60.03% combined, and therefore have a material influence on overall company performance.

Aggregate gross margins are relatively similar across channels, ranging from 62.17% to 63.59%. However, gross margin alone does not capture the full economics of each channel. Differences in customer acquisition, returns, fulfillment costs, discounting, customer retention, and working capital requirements may materially affect channel-level profitability.

Management should therefore evaluate channel opportunities using both revenue contribution and broader channel economics before allocating additional investment.

## 4. Geographic Performance

### Evidence

- The geographic data model distinguishes Region, Cluster, and Country, while the current geographic performance analysis reports country-level results within the regional hierarchy.
- The United Kingdom is the largest individual country by net revenue at £49,895.72, followed by India at £27,499.29, France at £22,063.38, and the United States at £21,633.43.
- The country-level results show variation in gross margin. Mexico records the highest gross margin in the current country-level output at 64.95%, followed by France at 63.48%, India at 63.05%, and the United Kingdom at 62.92%.
- Spain records the lowest gross margin at 59.32%, followed by Canada at 59.84% and the United Arab Emirates at 60.71%.
- Geographic scale and margin do not move uniformly together. For example, Mexico has a relatively small revenue contribution compared with the largest countries but records a comparatively high gross margin, while Spain has a smaller revenue contribution and a lower gross margin.
- Within Europe, countries span Northern Europe, Central Europe, and Southern Europe. Within North America, the analysis distinguishes the US & Canada and Mexico. Emerging Markets are represented by Latin America, South Asia, Southern Africa, and the Middle East.

### Interpretation

Geographic performance varies materially across Aurevia's country footprint in both revenue scale and gross margin. The United Kingdom represents a substantially larger revenue base than most other individual countries, while India, France, and the United States also represent significant country-level contributors.

The results also show that revenue scale does not necessarily correspond to gross-margin performance. Smaller markets can exhibit comparatively strong margins, while some lower-revenue markets exhibit weaker margins.

The cluster structure provides an additional analytical layer for identifying whether country-level differences are concentrated within particular geographic groupings. However, the current analysis reports country-level results rather than aggregated cluster or regional totals, so further analysis would be required before drawing conclusions about overall cluster or regional performance.

### Management implication

Management should evaluate geographic performance using both revenue contribution and margin rather than relying on country size alone.

The largest markets warrant attention because changes in their performance can have a material effect on enterprise results. At the same time, smaller markets with comparatively strong margins may warrant investigation to understand whether their economics can be sustained or whether there are opportunities to expand selectively.

Markets with lower gross margins should be investigated further to determine whether differences arise from product mix, pricing, discounting, channel mix, customer mix, or other operating factors. The geographic analysis therefore identifies where management should investigate further, but does not by itself establish the underlying causes of country-level performance differences.

## 10. Target vs Actual Performance

### Evidence

The target-versus-actual analysis is implemented in `09_target_vs_actual.sql`.

The target table contains:

- 1,257 target records
- 48 target months
- Target period: January 2022 through December 2025
- Full alignment with the 48 months for which sales data is available
- Unique target combinations at the target-date × division × region × channel grain

The target model provides revenue, gross-profit, and order targets at the monthly division × region × channel level.

The current target methodology is:

> **Target = simulated actual baseline × 1.05**

Revenue and gross-profit targets are based on a 5% uplift over the relevant simulated actual baseline. Order targets apply a 5% uplift to the corresponding baseline order count.

The SQL aggregates actual sales at the month × division × region × channel grain before joining to `fact_targets`. This prevents the country-level fanout that would occur if actuals were aggregated below the target grain and then joined only on region.

### Interpretation

The target-versus-actual analysis demonstrates how Aurevia can compare actual performance against an explicit planning benchmark across time and business dimensions.

However, the current targets are **synthetic retrospective benchmarks**, not historical management targets, approved budgets, or independently established forecasts.

Because the target values are deliberately derived from the simulated actual baseline using a 5% uplift, negative actual-versus-target variances are expected by construction. The resulting variance therefore should not be interpreted as evidence that Aurevia historically missed management commitments.

The primary value of this analysis within Project 1 is demonstrating a reproducible target-performance framework that can be extended to independently established management targets in future planning scenarios.

### Management implication

Management should use target-versus-actual analysis to identify performance gaps and prioritize investigation, but target quality is dependent on how targets are established.

A more mature Aurevia planning framework would establish management targets independently using strategic priorities, historical trends, operational drivers, pricing and volume assumptions, market context, and management expectations.

## Cross-Analysis Findings

The individual analyses provide a consistent view of Aurevia's commercial and economic performance. The most decision-relevant insights emerge when revenue, gross profit, margins, discounting, concentration, geography, customer behavior, returns, and target performance are considered together.

### 1. Aurevia's recent revenue weakness has not been accompanied by a broad deterioration in gross margin

Aurevia's net revenue declined from £47,770.47 in 2022 to £44,587.92 in 2024 before recovering modestly to £44,977.02 in 2025. Gross profit followed a similar pattern, while gross margin remained broadly stable at approximately 62.4%–62.5% throughout the period.

This indicates that the change in enterprise revenue scale was not accompanied by a material deterioration in aggregate gross-margin performance.

**Management insight:** The evidence does not indicate an enterprise-wide breakdown in gross-margin economics. Management should instead investigate where revenue has contracted or recovered and whether the areas generating growth are also generating attractive economic returns.

---

### 2. Gross-profit contribution is concentrated among a relatively small number of products and customers

Wellness generates the largest division-level gross profit at £40,782.88, while Retail generates the largest channel-level gross profit at £40,747.87.

At the product level, the five largest products by gross profit generate approximately £75,806.75, representing approximately 66.4% of total company gross profit.

Customer profitability is also concentrated. The five largest customers by gross profit generate approximately £51,623.21, representing approximately 45.2% of total company gross profit.

The evidence therefore shows that a relatively small number of products and customers account for a substantial share of Aurevia's economic contribution.

**Management insight:** Concentration creates both an opportunity and a dependency. Management should understand the resilience of major profit contributors, the operational exposure associated with them, and whether future growth would increase or reduce concentration.

---

### 3. Margin percentage and absolute gross-profit contribution provide different views of performance

Home & Lifestyle has the highest division-level gross margin at 63.81%, while Wellness generates the largest absolute gross profit at £40,782.88 with a 63.18% margin.

Similarly, Specialty has the highest channel-level gross margin at 63.59%, while Retail generates substantially more gross profit at £40,747.87 because of its larger revenue base.

This demonstrates the distinction between:

- margin percentage,
- revenue scale, and
- absolute gross-profit contribution.

A higher gross-margin percentage does not necessarily translate into greater absolute gross-profit contribution.

**Management insight:** Management should evaluate commercial opportunities using both margin quality and economic scale. Gross margin alone is insufficient for determining where additional attention or investment may create the greatest enterprise-level impact.

---

### 4. Discounting is relatively consistent at aggregate level, but varies meaningfully across division-channel combinations

Division-level discount rates range from 4.29% to 4.96%, while channel-level discount rates range from 4.40% to 4.77%.

Wellness and Personal Care have the highest division-level discount rates at 4.96%. At channel level, Distributors have the highest discount rate at 4.77%, followed by Retail at 4.69%.

The profitability evidence does not support treating discounting as the sole explanation for margin differences.

**Management insight:** Discounting should be evaluated in commercial context rather than treated as an isolated problem. Management should investigate whether the incremental revenue or strategic value associated with discounts is sufficient to justify the reduction in revenue realization.

---

### 5. The channel portfolio combines significant revenue concentration with relatively similar gross margins

Retail and Distributors collectively account for approximately 60.0% of company net revenue, making them central to enterprise performance.

Retail generates the largest channel gross profit at £40,747.87, while Distributors generate £27,553.72. Their gross margins are relatively similar at 62.17% and 62.20%, respectively.

The evidence therefore indicates that channel performance cannot be fully assessed through revenue and gross margin alone.

**Management insight:** Channel evaluation should combine revenue scale, gross-profit contribution, discounting, returns, and other channel economics that require further analysis, such as fulfillment and working-capital requirements.

---

### 6. Several economically important products also exhibit elevated return rates

Return rates vary materially across products, and some products combine substantial economic contribution with above-company-average return rates.

Daily Vitamin Complex generates £22,204.13 of net revenue and £13,781.63 of gross profit while recording a 2.67% return rate.

Scented Home Candle generates £23,182.31 of net revenue and £14,943.31 of gross profit while recording a 2.40% return rate.

Both products therefore combine meaningful economic contribution with return rates above the enterprise rate of 1.76%.

**Management insight:** Return investigations should consider both return rate and economic exposure. A high return rate on a major profit contributor may warrant a different level of investigation from the same rate on a smaller product.

---

### 7. Customer profitability is concentrated, while return behavior varies within the high-value customer base

The five largest customers by gross profit generate approximately 45.2% of total company gross profit.

Their return rates vary considerably. Maison & Vie has a 1.90% return rate, Wellness Boutique London 1.26%, Aurevia Online Store UK 2.28%, India Market Link 1.23%, and India Everyday Retail 1.96%.

This demonstrates that customer value and operational behavior are not uniform across the customer base.

**Management insight:** Strategic account management should combine customer value with operational indicators. High-value customers with unusual return behavior warrant targeted investigation into the underlying drivers before broader customer-policy changes are considered.

---

### 8. Geographic performance varies materially in both scale and operational outcomes

Europe generates the largest regional net revenue at £97,749.65 and approximately £61,162.95 of gross profit based on the country-level results. It also records the highest regional return rate at 1.90%.

At country level, Italy has a 3.00% return rate, Spain 2.79%, and Germany 2.14%, compared with the enterprise return rate of 1.76%.

The combination of market scale and operational variation means that geographic averages can conceal materially different commercial conditions within the portfolio.

**Management insight:** Geographic assessment should distinguish between market scale and market performance. Markets with significant economic contribution but elevated return rates may warrant targeted operational investigation, while smaller markets may require a different rationale for further growth or investment.

---

### 9. Returns are contained at enterprise level but create more concentrated exposure in specific areas

Aurevia's enterprise return rate is 1.76%, with £3,221.87 of refunds and an estimated gross-profit impact of £2,004.67.

However, return rates vary materially across products, customers, channels, divisions, and countries.

Return reasons are distributed across four categories: customer preference represents 27.55% of refund value, product quality 27.50%, incorrect item 23.71%, and damaged product 21.25%.

**Management insight:** Aurevia should avoid treating returns as a single enterprise-wide problem. Management should identify the product, customer, channel, geographic, and sales-cohort combinations associated with the greatest economic exposure and then investigate the underlying causes.

---

### 10. Target variance provides a framework for management performance monitoring

The target-versus-actual analysis provides a structured method for comparing actual revenue, gross profit, and order performance against explicit planning benchmarks.

The current targets cover 48 months and 1,257 target combinations across division, region, and channel.

However, these targets are synthetic retrospective benchmarks generated using a 5% uplift over the simulated actual baseline. Consequently, the resulting variances should not be interpreted as evidence of historical management underperformance.

**Management insight:** The target analysis demonstrates the analytical framework needed for management performance monitoring, while also highlighting the importance of target quality. Future management targets should be established independently using strategic and operational assumptions rather than retrospectively applying a fixed uplift to the same period's actual performance.

---

### 11. Aurevia's central management challenge is balancing growth, economic quality, concentration, and operational exposure

Taken together, the evidence indicates that Aurevia's performance cannot be understood through a single KPI.

The company maintains a stable enterprise gross margin of approximately 62.4%, but revenue scale weakened over the historical period before a modest recovery in 2025.

At the same time:

- gross-profit contribution is concentrated among a relatively small number of products and customers;
- Retail and Distributors account for approximately 60% of net revenue;
- discount rates vary across division-channel combinations;
- several economically important products and customers exhibit elevated return rates;
- geographic markets show materially different operational outcomes;
- higher gross-margin percentages do not necessarily correspond to the largest absolute gross-profit contributions; and
- target-versus-actual analysis provides a framework for future management performance monitoring, although the current targets are illustrative rather than independently established management commitments.

These findings create a set of interconnected management trade-offs rather than a single enterprise-wide performance problem.

**Management insight:** The next strategic question is not simply where Aurevia can generate more revenue, but where it can pursue sustainable growth while maintaining attractive economics and managing concentration and operational exposure.
