# Aurevia Group — Enterprise Performance Command Center

## Executive Summary

Aurevia Group experienced a decline in net revenue from 2022 through 2024, followed by a modest recovery in 2025. Net revenue decreased from £47,770.47 in 2022 to £44,587.92 in 2024 before increasing to £44,977.02 in 2025. Gross margin remained remarkably stable throughout the period, ranging from 62.40% to 62.50%. This indicates that the decline in gross profit broadly followed the change in revenue rather than reflecting a material deterioration in product-level gross economics.

Performance was not uniform across the business. Gross-profit contribution was concentrated among a relatively small number of products and customers, while divisions, channels, and geographic markets showed differences in revenue scale, profitability, discounting, and return exposure. The concentration of economic contribution means that changes in a limited number of commercially important relationships or products could have a disproportionate effect on overall performance.

Discounting was relatively consistent at the enterprise level, with Aurevia's overall discount rate at 4.65%. However, discounting varied across divisions and channels, with some division-channel combinations exhibiting materially higher discount rates. These differences warrant further investigation into whether discounting is supporting volume and customer value or reducing economic value without sufficient commercial benefit.

Returns were contained at the enterprise level, with an overall return rate of 1.76% and estimated gross-profit impact of approximately £2,004.67. However, return exposure was more concentrated in certain products, customers, channels, and geographies. This suggests that the aggregate return rate alone does not fully describe the operational exposure within the business.

Overall, Aurevia's performance challenge is not characterized by a broad deterioration in gross margin. Instead, management needs to understand the drivers of revenue scale, the concentration of economic contribution, the variation in discounting, and the areas where returns or other operational exposures are concentrated.

The analysis therefore points to a set of management questions rather than a single explanation for performance. In particular, management should investigate what is driving changes in revenue scale, how sustainable the current concentration of revenue and gross profit is, where discounting is economically justified, and which areas require deeper analysis before additional investment or corrective action.

## 1. Enterprise Performance

Aurevia's enterprise performance shows a decline in net revenue through 2024, followed by a modest recovery in 2025. Gross profit moved broadly in line with net revenue, while gross margin remained stable throughout the period.

### Interpretation

Net revenue declined by approximately 6.7% between 2022 and 2024, before increasing by 0.87% in 2025. Gross profit followed a similar pattern, declining through 2024 before increasing by approximately 1.01% in 2025.

Despite the change in revenue scale, gross margin remained within a narrow range of 62.40% to 62.50% across the four-year period. This indicates that the change in gross profit broadly reflected the change in revenue rather than a material deterioration in gross-margin percentage.

The enterprise-level results therefore point to a change in revenue scale rather than a broad deterioration in product-level gross economics. The analysis does not, by itself, establish the underlying causes of the revenue decline. These require examination of product, customer, channel, geographic, and other commercial patterns.

### Management implication

Management should distinguish between **why revenue scale changed** and **whether gross-margin economics deteriorated**. The enterprise results provide evidence for the latter: gross margin remained stable. The next level of analysis should therefore examine the products, customers, channels, and markets contributing to changes in revenue and gross-profit scale.

## 2. Growth & Profitability

Aurevia's revenue and gross-profit trends show that changes in profitability were broadly proportional to changes in revenue during the period. Revenue declined in both 2023 and 2024, followed by a modest recovery in 2025. Gross profit followed the same general pattern.

| Year | Net Revenue | Gross Profit | Revenue Growth | Gross Profit Growth |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2022 | £47,770.47 | £29,807.17 | — | — |
| 2023 | £45,632.27 | £28,496.07 | -4.48% | -4.40% |
| 2024 | £44,587.92 | £27,829.72 | -2.29% | -2.34% |
| 2025 | £44,977.02 | £28,112.02 | +0.87% | +1.01% |

### Interpretation

The decline in net revenue was accompanied by a broadly proportional decline in gross profit. In 2023, revenue decreased by 4.48% while gross profit decreased by 4.40%. In 2024, revenue decreased by 2.29% while gross profit decreased by 2.34%. In 2025, both measures returned to modest growth, with net revenue increasing by 0.87% and gross profit by 1.01%.

This pattern is consistent with the stable gross-margin results observed at the enterprise level. The available evidence does not indicate a broad deterioration in the percentage of revenue retained after product costs.

The results therefore distinguish between **growth performance** and **gross-margin performance**. Aurevia experienced pressure on revenue scale through 2024, but the proportion of revenue converted into gross profit remained broadly stable.

### Management implication

Management should investigate the factors affecting revenue scale separately from gross-margin economics. Understanding whether changes in revenue are associated with order volume, customer activity, product mix, channel performance, geographic performance, or other commercial factors is necessary before attributing the change to a specific driver.

## 3. Product & Customer Concentration

Aurevia's gross-profit contribution is concentrated among a relatively small number of products and customers. This creates an important distinction between overall business performance and the distribution of economic value within the portfolio.

The five products generating the highest gross profit contributed approximately 66.4% of total company gross profit. The top three products by net revenue accounted for approximately 45.8% of company net revenue, while the top five accounted for approximately 71.7%.

Product-level gross margins also varied. Reusable Storage Set recorded a margin of 64.63%, while Scented Home Candle recorded 64.46%. At the lower end, Multi-Surface Cleaner recorded 58.70%, Kitchen Paper Towels 59.64%, and Hydrating Body Wash 60.40%.

### Customer performance

Aurevia's customer base also shows meaningful concentration. The largest customer, Maison & Vie, contributed approximately 11.0% of company net revenue. Wellness Boutique London contributed 9.41%, Aurevia Online Store UK 9.16%, India Market Link 8.69%, and India Everyday Retail 6.34%.

Together, the five largest customers accounted for approximately 44.6% of company net revenue.

Customer profitability remained relatively consistent across the four customer segments. Growth customers generated £94,422.44 in net revenue and £58,931.94 in gross profit at a 62.41% gross margin. Core customers generated £37,896.05 in net revenue at a 62.70% margin, Strategic customers generated £27,618.28 at a 62.45% margin, and Emerging customers generated £23,028.72 at a 62.11% margin.

### Interpretation

The concentration analysis shows that Aurevia's overall economics depend disproportionately on a relatively small number of products and customers. This does not, by itself, indicate that the concentration is undesirable. However, it means that changes in commercially important products or customer relationships could have a material effect on enterprise performance.

The analysis also shows that high revenue contribution and high gross-margin percentage are not necessarily the same thing. A product can generate substantial gross profit because of its revenue scale even if its margin percentage is not the highest in the portfolio.

Similarly, customer segments have relatively similar gross margins despite meaningful differences in revenue contribution. This suggests that management should consider both the scale of economic contribution and the percentage margin when evaluating commercial priorities.

### Management implication

Management should monitor concentration among economically important products and customers and distinguish between **scale of contribution** and **margin percentage**. Further analysis should examine the sustainability of major customer relationships, the performance of high-contribution products, and whether concentration creates commercial or operational exposure.

## 4. Channel & Geographic Performance

Aurevia's revenue is distributed across five channels, with Retail and Distributors representing the largest combined share of net revenue. Channel gross margins are relatively similar, although the scale of revenue contribution differs materially.

Retail and Distributors together accounted for approximately 60.03% of total net revenue. Gross margins across the five channels ranged from 62.17% to 63.59%, indicating relatively limited variation in margin percentage despite differences in revenue scale.

At the division-channel level, several commercially significant combinations emerged. Everyday Essentials generated £21,162.85 in net revenue through Retail, while Wellness generated £21,148.80 through Retail. Wellness also generated £13,208.89 through Distributors and £12,823.75 through Specialty.

### Geographic performance

Aurevia's geographic results also vary materially across markets. Europe generated approximately £61,162.95 in gross profit based on the country-level analysis, while North America and Emerging Markets contributed smaller amounts. Country-level results provide additional evidence of variation within these regional groups.

Geographic performance also differs in operational exposure. For example, the overall return rate was 1.90% in Europe, 1.63% in Emerging Markets, and 1.56% in North America. At country level, Italy recorded a 3.00% return rate, Spain 2.79%, and Germany 2.14%.

### Interpretation

The channel analysis shows that Aurevia's revenue base is concentrated in a small number of channels, while gross-margin percentages remain relatively consistent across the channel portfolio. This means that channel scale is more differentiated than channel gross-margin performance.

The geographic analysis similarly shows that aggregate regional performance can conceal meaningful differences between individual countries. Geographic scale and operational outcomes therefore need to be considered together rather than relying solely on regional totals.

The current analysis identifies these differences but does not establish the underlying causes of geographic or channel performance. Further investigation would be required to determine the contribution of factors such as customer mix, product mix, pricing, discounting, market conditions, or operating costs.

### Management implication

Management should monitor the concentration of revenue across Retail and Distributor channels while assessing whether the current channel mix provides sufficient diversification. Geographic performance should be reviewed at country level where material differences exist, particularly where revenue contribution and operational exposure diverge.

## 5. Discounting & Revenue Realization

Aurevia generated £191,888.18 in gross revenue across the analysis period and provided £8,920.50 in discounts, resulting in £182,967.68 in net revenue. The enterprise discount rate was 4.65%, corresponding to a revenue realization rate of 95.35%.

### Enterprise and division performance

|---|---:|
| Wellness | 4.96% |
| Personal Care | 4.96% |
| Everyday Essentials | 4.35% |
| Home & Lifestyle | 4.29% |

Wellness and Personal Care recorded the highest division-level discount rates at 4.96%, while Home & Lifestyle recorded the lowest at 4.29%.

### Channel performance

| Channel | Discount Rate |
|---|---:|
| Distributors | 4.77% |
| Retail | 4.69% |
| E-commerce | 4.64% |
| Specialty | 4.55% |
| Direct-to-Consumer | 4.40% |

Discounting also varied across division-channel combinations. Personal Care through Specialty recorded the highest observed discount rate at 5.75%, followed by Personal Care through E-commerce at 5.65%. Personal Care through E-commerce also recorded a gross margin of 59.99%.

### Interpretation

At the enterprise level, Aurevia's discount rate was relatively contained at 4.65%. However, the variation across divisions, channels, and division-channel combinations indicates that discounting is not uniform throughout the business.

Higher discounting does not automatically indicate poor commercial performance. Discounts may be used to support customer acquisition, volume, channel relationships, inventory movement, or other commercial objectives. The current analysis does not establish whether individual discounts generated sufficient incremental economic benefit to justify the revenue given up.

Revenue realization is mechanically related to the discount rate because both measures are derived from gross revenue and discounts. Consequently, differences in realization should be interpreted primarily as differences in the level of discounting rather than as an independent measure of commercial effectiveness.

### Management implication

Management should investigate the purpose and economic effectiveness of higher-discount activities rather than treating discount rate alone as evidence of underperformance. Further analysis should connect discounting with volume, customer behavior, product performance, and profitability to determine whether discounts are creating sufficient incremental value.

## 6. Returns & Operational Exposure

Aurevia recorded 13,504 units sold and 237 returned units across the analysis period, resulting in an overall return rate of 1.76%. Total refunds amounted to £3,221.87. Based on estimated product cost associated with returned units, the estimated gross-profit impact was £2,004.67, equivalent to approximately 1.10% of total net revenue.

### Product and division exposure

Several products recorded higher return rates than the enterprise average. Multi-Surface Cleaner and Daily Vitamin Complex each recorded a 2.67% return rate, followed by Scented Home Candle at 2.40% and Nourishing Hand Cream at 2.39%.

By division, Home & Lifestyle recorded the highest return rate at 2.27%, followed by Wellness at 1.81%, Personal Care at 1.72%, and Everyday Essentials at 1.35%.

### Channel and customer exposure

Retail recorded the largest number of returned units, with 87 returned units. Direct-to-Consumer recorded the highest channel-level return rate at 2.51%, followed by Specialty at 2.04%. E-commerce recorded a 1.35% return rate, Retail 1.74%, and Distributors 1.57%.

Return behavior also varied across customers. Andes Consumer Supply recorded a 4.37% return rate, Aurevia Online Store Germany 3.76%, and Urban Health Collective 3.23%.

### Geographic exposure

Return rates also varied geographically. Europe recorded a 1.90% return rate, Emerging Markets 1.63%, and North America 1.56%. At country level, Italy recorded the highest observed rate at 3.00%, followed by Spain at 2.79% and Germany at 2.14%.

The analysis of return reasons showed that customer preference represented 27.55% of refund value, product quality 27.50%, incorrect item 23.71%, and damaged product 21.25%.

Return rates also varied across monthly sales cohorts, with notable peaks in March 2023, April 2024, and February 2025. These observations indicate periods that may warrant further investigation, but they do not establish a sustained deterioration in return performance.

### Interpretation

Returns are contained at the enterprise level, but the aggregate rate masks more concentrated exposure within particular products, customers, channels, and geographies.

The distinction between return rate and economic exposure is important. A product or customer may have a relatively high return rate without representing the largest financial impact, while a high-volume product or customer with a moderate return rate may generate greater absolute exposure.

The analysis also indicates that return reasons are distributed across several categories rather than being dominated by a single cause. The current evidence therefore identifies areas for investigation but does not establish one overarching cause of returns.

### Management implication

Management should monitor return exposure at product, customer, channel, and country level rather than relying solely on the enterprise return rate. Higher-exposure areas should be investigated to determine whether targeted actions relating to product quality, fulfilment accuracy, damage prevention, or customer expectations could reduce avoidable returns.

## 7. Target vs Actual Performance

Project 1 also includes a target-versus-actual analysis to demonstrate how management could evaluate performance against an explicit planning benchmark.

The target dataset contains 1,257 target records covering 48 monthly periods from January 2022 through December 2025. The targets align with the full period for which sales data is available.

### Target methodology

The current targets are **illustrative planning benchmarks**, not historical management targets or approved budgets.

They were generated using a transparent synthetic methodology:

> Target = simulated actual baseline × 1.05

Revenue and gross-profit targets therefore represent a 5% uplift over the corresponding simulated actual baseline, while order targets apply a 5% uplift to the relevant baseline order count.

This methodology was selected to provide a reproducible benchmark for demonstrating target-versus-actual analysis within the fictional Aurevia environment. It should not be interpreted as a conventional management target-setting methodology.

### Interpretation

The target-versus-actual analysis provides a framework for identifying variance across month, division, region, and channel. Because the current targets are deliberately constructed from the same simulated actual baseline with a 5% uplift, the resulting negative variances are expected by design.

The analysis therefore demonstrates the mechanics of target tracking and variance analysis rather than establishing that Aurevia historically missed approved management targets.

The target analysis should be interpreted as a **portfolio analytics capability**: it shows how actual performance can be compared with a defined planning benchmark and how management could identify areas requiring further investigation.

### Management implication

A mature management-performance framework would distinguish between actual performance, forecasts, management targets, and external or internal benchmarks.

For future planning scenarios, Aurevia could introduce independently established management targets based on strategic priorities, historical performance, operational drivers, pricing and volume assumptions, market conditions, and management expectations.

The current target layer provides the technical foundation for that framework while maintaining a clear distinction between simulated benchmark methodology and genuine management planning assumptions.

## 8. Cross-Analysis

The individual analyses show that Aurevia's performance cannot be explained by a single enterprise-level KPI. The business experienced a decline in revenue scale through 2024 while maintaining stable gross-margin economics, and the distribution of value across products, customers, channels, and markets is uneven.

Several themes emerge when the analyses are considered together.

### 8.1 Revenue pressure without broad margin deterioration

Aurevia's net revenue declined through 2024, while gross margin remained within a narrow range of 62.40% to 62.50%. Gross profit therefore moved broadly in proportion to revenue.

This suggests that the immediate management question is not simply whether Aurevia is becoming less profitable at the product level, but what is affecting the scale and composition of revenue.

### 8.2 Economic contribution is concentrated

A relatively small number of products and customers account for a substantial share of Aurevia's revenue and gross profit. The five products generating the highest gross profit contributed approximately 66.4% of total gross profit, while the five largest customers represented approximately 44.6% of net revenue.

This concentration creates areas of material commercial importance that warrant continued management attention.

### 8.3 Margin percentage and economic contribution are different measures

The analyses show that the products and channels with the highest gross-margin percentages are not necessarily those contributing the largest absolute amount of gross profit.

Management should therefore consider both **margin percentage** and **absolute economic contribution** when assessing performance.

### 8.4 Discounting varies across the portfolio

The enterprise discount rate was 4.65%, but discounting varied across divisions, channels, and division-channel combinations.

This creates a potential area for deeper investigation. However, the current analysis does not establish whether higher discounts are economically inefficient because the commercial purpose and incremental benefits of those discounts have not been measured.

### 8.5 Channel concentration and margin similarity

Retail and Distributors accounted for approximately 60.03% of net revenue, while channel gross margins remained relatively similar.

This means that Aurevia's channel exposure is driven more by differences in revenue scale than by large differences in gross-margin percentage.

### 8.6 Returns create concentrated rather than broad exposure

The enterprise return rate was 1.76%, but higher return rates were observed for specific products, customers, channels, and countries.

The financial significance of returns should therefore be assessed using both return rates and absolute economic exposure rather than relying on the enterprise rate alone.

### 8.7 Geographic performance requires deeper examination

Regional and country-level results show meaningful variation in both commercial scale and return exposure. The geographic analysis identifies areas that warrant further investigation but does not establish the underlying causes of those differences.

### 8.8 Target variance provides a management-performance framework

The target-versus-actual analysis demonstrates how Aurevia could monitor performance against defined planning benchmarks across time and business dimensions.

However, because the current targets are synthetic 5% uplift benchmarks derived from simulated actuals, the observed variance should not be interpreted as evidence of historical management underperformance.

The analysis instead establishes the technical and analytical foundation for future management-target scenarios in which targets could be independently established using strategic, operational, and market-based assumptions.

### Overall interpretation

Taken together, the evidence suggests that Aurevia's central performance challenge is not a broad collapse in gross-margin economics. Instead, management needs to understand the factors affecting revenue scale while managing concentration, discounting variation, operational exposure, and performance against defined planning benchmarks.

The analysis therefore provides a diagnostic foundation for subsequent strategic work. It identifies where management attention may be required while distinguishing established evidence from questions that require deeper analysis.

## 9. Management Priorities

The analysis identifies several areas that warrant management attention. These priorities are based on the evidence available in Project 1 and are intended to guide further investigation rather than prescribe a single course of action.

### 9.1 Understand the drivers of revenue performance

Net revenue declined through 2024 before recovering modestly in 2025, while gross margin remained stable. Management should therefore investigate the factors affecting revenue scale, including changes in customers, products, channels, geographic markets, order activity, and product mix.

### 9.2 Protect economically important products and customers

A significant proportion of gross profit and net revenue is concentrated among a relatively small number of products and customers. Management should monitor these relationships and assess their sustainability, particularly where commercially important products or customers also show elevated operational exposure.

### 9.3 Review discounting where variation is highest

Enterprise discounting is relatively contained, but certain division-channel combinations show materially higher discount rates. Management should understand the commercial purpose and incremental benefit of these discounts before determining whether changes are warranted.

### 9.4 Monitor channel and geographic concentration

Retail and Distributors account for approximately 60.03% of net revenue, while geographic performance varies across countries. Management should consider the implications of this concentration and monitor areas where commercial scale and operational exposure are closely linked.

### 9.5 Investigate concentrated return exposure

The enterprise return rate is relatively contained, but higher return rates occur within specific products, customers, channels, and countries. Management should identify whether these concentrations reflect recurring product, fulfilment, customer-expectation, or other operational issues.

### 9.6 Use target variance as a management-performance framework

Target-versus-actual analysis can provide a useful framework for identifying performance gaps and prioritizing management investigation. However, the current targets are illustrative benchmarks rather than independently established management commitments.

Future management planning should use targets based on explicit strategic and operational assumptions rather than relying solely on retrospective uplift from actual performance.

### 9.7 Preserve the distinction between evidence and diagnosis

Project 1 identifies where performance differs, but not every observed difference has an established cause. Management should avoid treating correlation or concentration as proof of causation and use targeted follow-up analysis where the evidence is insufficient to support a decision.

## 10. Areas Requiring Further Investigation

Project 1 provides a diagnostic view of Aurevia's performance, but several questions require deeper analysis before management can determine specific corrective or investment actions.

### Revenue drivers

- What specifically drove the decline in net revenue between 2022 and 2024?
- To what extent were changes in order volume, units, customer activity, product mix, channel mix, or geographic performance responsible?
- Is the modest 2025 recovery broad-based or concentrated in particular parts of the business?

### Customer and product concentration

- How sustainable are the revenue and gross-profit contributions of the largest customers and products?
- Are high-value customers growing, stable, or declining?
- Do concentrated products or customers also create supply, retention, or operational risks?

### Discount effectiveness

- Which discounts generate incremental volume or customer value?
- Are higher discount rates associated with sufficient increases in sales or customer activity?
- Which discounting patterns should be tested further rather than changed immediately?

### Channel economics

- How do channels differ when costs beyond product cost are considered?
- Does the current channel mix remain attractive after logistics, fulfilment, customer acquisition, and other channel-specific costs?
- Are the largest channels also the most strategically important for future growth?

### Geographic performance

- What explains differences between countries within the same region?
- Are differences driven by market conditions, customer mix, product mix, pricing, distribution, or operational factors?
- Which markets warrant deeper market-entry or growth analysis?

### Returns

- What operational or commercial factors explain higher return rates in specific products, customers, channels, and countries?
- Which return reasons are potentially preventable?
- What would be the economic benefit of reducing returns in the highest-exposure areas?

### Target-setting and planning

- What management targets would Aurevia set independently of historical actual performance?
- Which strategic, operational, pricing, volume, margin, and market assumptions should inform those targets?
- How would actual performance compare with an independently established management plan rather than a retrospective illustrative benchmark?

### Financial performance beyond gross profit

Project 1 focuses on revenue, product cost, gross profit, and gross margin. A full operating-profit or EBITDA analysis is outside the current scope because the Project 1 model does not yet contain the detailed operating-expense structure required for a robust calculation.

These questions provide natural inputs into later portfolio projects, including customer growth, pricing, supply chain, investment, and risk analysis.

## Appendix — KPI Definitions & Methodology

### KPI definitions

| KPI | Definition |
|---|---|
| Gross Revenue | Total sales value before discounts |
| Discounts | Value of discounts applied to gross revenue |
| Net Revenue | Gross Revenue minus Discounts |
| Product Cost | Direct product cost associated with units sold |
| Gross Profit | Net Revenue minus Product Cost |
| Gross Margin | Gross Profit divided by Net Revenue |
| Revenue Growth | Percentage change in Net Revenue compared with the prior year |
| Gross Profit Growth | Percentage change in Gross Profit compared with the prior year |
| Discount Rate | Discounts divided by Gross Revenue |
| Revenue Realization | Net Revenue divided by Gross Revenue |
| Return Rate | Returned Units divided by Units Sold |
| Active Customers | Distinct customers generating sales during the relevant period |

### Target and variance definitions

| KPI | Definition |
|---|---|
| Target Revenue | Illustrative planning benchmark for revenue |
| Target Gross Profit | Illustrative planning benchmark for gross profit |
| Target Orders | Illustrative planning benchmark for order volume |
| Revenue Variance | Actual Revenue minus Target Revenue |
| Revenue Variance % | Revenue Variance divided by Target Revenue |
| Gross Profit Variance | Actual Gross Profit minus Target Gross Profit |
| Gross Profit Variance % | Gross Profit Variance divided by Target Gross Profit |
| Order Variance | Actual segment-level order count minus Target Orders |
| Order Variance % | Order Variance divided by Target Orders |

The current target dataset is constructed using a 5% uplift over the relevant simulated actual baseline. These targets are intended as illustrative planning benchmarks and should not be interpreted as historical management targets or approved budgets.

### Analytical methodology

Project 1 uses a combination of SQL-based aggregation, comparative analysis, concentration analysis, target-versus-actual variance analysis, and cross-dimensional performance analysis.

The analysis evaluates performance across multiple business dimensions, including:

- Time
- Division
- Product
- Customer
- Channel
- Region
- Country
- Discounting
- Returns
- Targets

Enterprise-level performance is calculated from the underlying sales and product-cost data. Revenue and gross-profit measures are aggregated independently before ratios such as gross margin and growth rates are calculated.

Target-versus-actual analysis aggregates actual sales at the month × division × region × channel grain before joining to the corresponding target records. This avoids duplicating actuals when target records are matched across the same dimensional grain.

Return analysis uses returned units relative to total units sold. Return-related financial exposure is estimated using the product cost associated with returned units.

### Interpretation principles

The analysis distinguishes between:

1. **Descriptive evidence** — what the data shows.

2. **Interpretation** — what the observed pattern may indicate.

3. **Management implication** — what question or area of attention follows from the evidence.

Observed relationships are not treated as proof of causation. Where the available data does not establish why a performance difference occurred, the issue is identified as an area requiring further investigation.

Target-versus-actual results are also interpreted in the context of the target methodology. Because the current targets are synthetic retrospective benchmarks, the variance results demonstrate analytical technique rather than independently validated historical management performance.

### Scope limitations

Project 1 focuses on commercial performance and product-level economics through revenue, discounts, product cost, gross profit, gross margin, targets, and returns.

The analysis does not currently include a full operating-expense structure, EBITDA, operating profit, or cash-flow analysis. These measures require additional financial data and are therefore outside the current Project 1 scope.

The target dataset is synthetic and designed for analytical demonstration. It does not represent approved budgets, historical management commitments, or independently validated forecasts.

The analysis is also primarily diagnostic. It identifies performance patterns, concentrations, target variances, and areas requiring management attention but does not attempt to establish causal relationships where the available data is insufficient.
