# Project 2 — Market Entry Strategy
## Evidence Architecture and Source Register

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the evidence architecture that will support Aurevia
Group's Project 2 market-entry assessment.

Its purpose is to establish:

- What evidence is required
- Which indicators will be collected
- How each indicator will be defined
- Which source types should be prioritized
- What observation periods should be used
- How evidence quality will be assessed
- How sources will be recorded and traced
- How external evidence will connect to the market-screening analysis
- How evidence will later support the investment model

The objective is to create a reproducible evidence base rather than a
collection of disconnected market facts.

---

## 2. Relationship to the Project 2 Analytical Framework

The evidence architecture follows the analytical sequence established in
the Market Screening Framework:

```text
Candidate Universe
        ↓
Eligibility
        ↓
Evidence Collection
        ↓
Market Screening
        ↓
Shortlist
        ↓
Deep Market Analysis
        ↓
Entry Economics
        ↓
Risk & Scenario Analysis
        ↓
Investment Decision
```

The evidence collected during the screening stage must therefore be
designed with later investment analysis in mind.

A metric should not be collected merely because it is available.

It should have a defined analytical purpose.

## 3. Evidence Architecture

Project 2 evidence will be organized into six major evidence domains:

1. Market Opportunity
2. Economic Environment
3. Consumer Potential
4. Competitive Environment
5. Strategic Fit
6. Entry Feasibility and Risk

Additional evidence will support the subsequent investment case:

7. Entry Strategy
8. Financial Model Inputs
9. Scenario and Risk Inputs

The first six domains support market screening.

The final three domains become increasingly important after the shortlist
has been established.

## 4. Evidence Hierarchy

Evidence will be prioritized according to source quality and relevance.

### Tier 1 — Primary and authoritative sources

Preferred sources include:

- National statistical agencies
- Central banks
- Government ministries
- Government regulators
- Official trade and investment agencies
- International organizations
- Official company filings and annual reports
- Official corporate investor-relations publications

These sources should be preferred for:

- Macroeconomic indicators
- Population
- Demographics
- Regulatory requirements
- Trade and investment conditions
- Official company information
- Reported competitor performance

### Tier 2 — High-quality analytical sources

Preferred sources include:

- Established industry research
- Specialist market research
- Reputable financial institutions
- Established consulting research
- Credible sector organizations
- Recognized economic research institutions

These sources may support:

- Category size
- Category growth
- Consumer trends
- Industry structure
- Competitive analysis
- Market forecasts

Methodology and definitions must be reviewed before using estimates.

### Tier 3 — Reputable business and trade sources

Examples include:

- Established financial press
- Reputable business publications
- Industry trade publications
- Recognized professional associations

These sources may support:

- Competitor developments
- Market-entry activity
- Distribution developments
- Strategic context
- Recent industry events

They should generally supplement rather than replace primary evidence
for material quantitative claims.

### Tier 4 — Discovery sources

Examples include:

- Search-result snippets
- Unsourced blogs
- General aggregators
- Informal commentary
- Secondary summaries without clear methodology

These sources may be used to discover potential leads.

They should not independently support material investment claims.

## 6. Evidence Quality Assessment

Evidence quality will be assessed using four levels.

### High

Evidence is:

- From a highly authoritative source
- Directly relevant to the analytical question
- Recent enough for the intended use
- Clearly defined
- Geographically appropriate
- Methodologically transparent
- Comparable with the other evidence being used

### Medium

Evidence is useful but has one or more limitations.

Examples:

- Older publication
- Secondary source
- Limited geographic detail
- Methodological limitations
- Forecast uncertainty
- Partial category coverage

### Low

Evidence may provide useful context but should not independently support
a material investment conclusion.

Examples:

- Weakly documented estimates
- Limited methodology
- Older secondary commentary
- Narrow or uncertain coverage

### Insufficient

Evidence is not reliable enough for the intended analytical purpose.

It may remain in the research log as a lead but should not be used as
material decision evidence.

## 17. Observation-Period Strategy

Project 2 will use a layered time structure.

### Historical period

Where data availability permits, the analysis should examine approximately
2020–2025 for historical context.

The actual period used may vary by indicator depending on data availability.

### Current position

The most recent reliable observation available at the time of research
should be recorded separately from historical observations.

### Forward outlook

Where credible forecasts exist, the analysis may incorporate approximately
2026–2030 outlooks.

Forecast periods and publication dates must be recorded.

### Aurevia planning horizon

Aurevia's broader portfolio planning horizon remains 2022–2026.

Project 2 may extend beyond 2026 for market and investment modelling where
forward-looking evidence is required.

Any extension must be clearly labelled.

- The interpretation relevant to Aurevia

## 20. Evidence Quality Controls

Before evidence enters the analytical dataset, it must pass appropriate quality checks.

The review should assess:

- **Definition:** Does the evidence measure the intended concept?
- **Period:** Is the observation period clear and appropriate?
- **Geography:** Does the evidence refer to the target country or a broader area?
- **Unit:** Are units and currencies clearly identified?
- **Comparability:** Can the measure be compared across candidate markets?
- **Source quality:** Is the source authoritative and methodologically credible?
- **Recency:** Is the evidence sufficiently current for the decision?
- **Methodology:** Is the calculation or estimation method documented?
- **Limitations:** Are material caveats recorded?
- **Decision relevance:** Could the evidence materially affect screening or investment analysis?

Evidence that fails a critical check must not be silently treated as reliable.

It may be excluded, retained with an explicit limitation, or investigated further, depending on its importance to the decision.

## 22. Research-to-Data Pipeline

Research evidence will move through a controlled pipeline before it is used in a decision model.

```text
Public and Authoritative Sources
              ↓
         Source Register
              ↓
          Raw Evidence
              ↓
        Data Extraction
              ↓
    Cleaning and Standardization
              ↓
           Validation
              ↓
      Analytical Dataset
              ↓
         Python / SQL
              ↓
       Market Screening
              ↓
            Shortlist
              ↓
       Investment Analysis
```

Each stage has a distinct purpose.

- **Source register:** Records where evidence came from and how it will be used.
- **Raw evidence:** Preserves source material or extracted values before analytical transformation.
- **Data extraction:** Converts relevant source information into a usable format.
- **Cleaning and standardization:** Harmonizes definitions, units, currencies, periods, and geographic labels where appropriate.
- **Validation:** Checks completeness, consistency, comparability, and calculation accuracy.
- **Analytical dataset:** Contains validated evidence prepared for screening and deeper analysis.
- **Python and SQL:** Support reproducible data preparation, analysis, and calculation.
- **Market screening:** Compares eligible candidate markets using documented evidence.
- **Investment analysis:** Evaluates the shortlisted markets using deeper commercial, strategic, financial, and risk analysis.

Transformations must be documented so that material results can be reproduced from the underlying evidence.

## 23. External Evidence and Aurevia Internal Data

The analysis will use two broad evidence sources.

### External evidence

External evidence describes the market environment outside Aurevia, including:

- Market size and growth
- Economic and demographic conditions
- Consumer behavior and purchasing power
- Competitors and market structure
- Regulation and market-access requirements
- Distribution infrastructure
- Country and currency risks

External evidence must be supported by identifiable sources or explicitly labelled as an estimate.

### Aurevia internal data

Internal data describes Aurevia's own commercial and operational position, including:

- Product portfolio and product economics
- Historical sales and gross-profit performance
- Customer and channel relationships
- Existing geographic presence
- Operational and distribution capabilities
- Existing supplier and logistics arrangements
- Available investment resources, where defined

Project 1 provides an initial simulated commercial performance foundation. It does not automatically provide every financial or operational input required for a market-entry decision.

Where internal evidence is unavailable, the analysis must identify the gap and determine whether it can be addressed through a defensible assumption, additional research, or a clearly stated limitation.

External market attractiveness must not be confused with Aurevia's ability to compete successfully in that market.

---

## 24. Treatment of Estimates

Not every required input will be directly observable. Estimates may be necessary, but their status must remain transparent.

Use the following classifications where applicable:

- **Reported value:** Directly reported by an identified source.
- **Derived value:** Calculated from documented source data using a stated method.
- **Published estimate:** Estimated by an external organization.
- **Published forecast:** A forward-looking estimate produced by an identified source.
- **Aurevia assumption:** A management-style assumption introduced for the fictional business.
- **Analytical scenario:** A value used to examine a possible future condition rather than to assert that it will occur.

These categories must not be treated as interchangeable.

For example, a published market-growth forecast is not a guaranteed outcome, and an Aurevia revenue assumption is not observed market evidence.

Every material estimate must have a documented rationale, a clear intended use, and an appropriate uncertainty treatment.

---

## 25. Treatment of Conflicting Sources

Different sources may report different values for apparently similar measures.

Differences may arise from:

- Different category definitions
- Different geographic boundaries
- Different observation periods
- Different currencies or conversion dates
- Different measurement methods
- Different coverage of formal and informal activity
- Revisions to historical statistics
- Differences between reported results and forecasts

When conflicting evidence is identified, the analyst should first determine whether the sources are measuring the same concept.

Where the measures are comparable, assess source authority, methodology, recency, and relevance to the decision.

If a defensible preferred value can be selected, document the reason for selecting it.

If the conflict cannot be resolved, preserve the uncertainty and consider using a range or scenario analysis where it could materially affect the decision.

A source must never be selected merely because it produces a more favorable market-entry case.

---

## 26. AI-Assisted Research Controls

AI may support research efficiency and analytical preparation, but it must not replace source verification or professional judgment.

Permitted uses include:

- Discovering potentially relevant sources
- Organizing source material
- Summarizing identified publications
- Comparing documented findings
- Identifying inconsistencies or missing evidence
- Generating hypotheses for further investigation
- Drafting research notes and executive summaries for review

AI-generated claims are not authoritative evidence.

Material factual claims must be verified against the underlying source, and the source must be assessed for credibility and relevance.

The project will maintain an AI-use register recording material uses of AI, including the task performed, the output used, the validation undertaken, and any limitations identified.

Confidential credentials, private information, and sensitive data must not be entered into external AI tools.

AI-assisted research must preserve traceability, expose uncertainty, and leave final analytical and investment judgments under human review.

## 27. Research Completion Criteria

Research for a candidate market or evidence domain is ready for analytical use when:

- The required indicators have been identified.
- Material indicators have clear definitions and units.
- Relevant sources have been located and recorded.
- Observation periods and geographic coverage are documented.
- Source quality has been assessed.
- Important data gaps have been identified.
- Conflicting values have been investigated or explicitly retained as uncertainty.
- External evidence is distinguished from assumptions and analytical estimates.
- Comparability across candidate markets has been assessed.
- Material claims can be traced to their underlying sources.
- Limitations that could affect the decision are documented.

Completion does not require every possible data point to be available.

It requires sufficient reliable evidence to support the intended analysis, with material gaps and uncertainties made explicit.

## 28. Transition to Market Screening

Once the initial evidence has been collected and validated, the project will move from research architecture into comparative market analysis.

The transition will follow this sequence:

```text
Evidence Collection
        ↓
Evidence Validation
        ↓
Definition and Unit Standardization
        ↓
Candidate Market Profiles
        ↓
Cross-Market Comparison
        ↓
Red-Flag Review
        ↓
Eligibility Assessment
        ↓
Preliminary Shortlist
        ↓
Decision on Deeper Investment Analysis
```

The initial objective is to establish which eligible markets warrant further investigation.

It is not to manufacture a winner from incomplete evidence or to make an investment decision solely from a screening table.

A comparative scoring model may be introduced if it improves transparency and decision quality. Its use, weights, and interpretation must be justified rather than assumed in advance.

After screening, shortlisted markets will receive deeper analysis of market attractiveness, competitive position, Aurevia's strategic fit, feasible entry options, investment economics, and risk-adjusted outcomes.

The screening results are an input to the investment decision, not a substitute for it.

## 29. Governing Principle

The purpose of the evidence architecture is to ensure that the market-entry recommendation is based on relevant, credible, traceable, and appropriately interpreted information.

The project will prioritize:

- Evidence that can change the decision over data collected merely for completeness
- Clear definitions over superficially comparable numbers
- Traceable sources over unsupported claims
- Explicit assumptions over hidden estimates
- Transparent uncertainty over false precision
- Reproducible analysis over undocumented calculations
- Strategic relevance over unnecessary analytical complexity

Every material number should have a reason for inclusion. Every material claim should be traceable. Every important assumption should be visible. Every recommendation should be open to challenge and capable of being reproduced from the underlying evidence.

The objective is not to accumulate the largest possible research dataset. It is to build a defensible chain from evidence to analysis, from analysis to insight, and from insight to an investment decision.
