# Project 2 — Market Entry Strategy
## Decision Charter

### 1. Project Purpose

Evaluate potential international expansion opportunities for Aurevia Group
and determine whether entering a shortlisted market would create sufficient
risk-adjusted value to justify the investment.

### 2. Executive Question

Which potential market offers the strongest combination of commercial
opportunity, strategic fit, financial viability, and manageable risk—and
should Aurevia enter, delay entry, or not enter?

### 3. Business Context

Aurevia Group is a fictional global consumer and retail company headquartered
in London, United Kingdom.

Its divisions are:

- Personal Care
- Home & Lifestyle
- Wellness
- Everyday Essentials

Its commercial channels are:

- Retail
- E-commerce
- Distributors
- Direct-to-Consumer
- Specialty

The company is considering further international expansion. The project
will assess the attractiveness and feasibility of potential markets without
assuming in advance that expansion is desirable.

### 4. Decisions to Support

Decision 1: Determine which markets merit deeper investigation.

Decision 2: Assess whether investment in a shortlisted market is justified.

Decision 3: If entry is recommended, identify a suitable entry approach,
investment requirements, key conditions, and implementation priorities.

### 5. Market-Screening Dimensions

- Market opportunity
- Economic environment
- Consumer potential
- Competitive environment
- Strategic fit
- Entry feasibility and risk

Market screening will use documented evidence and explicit eligibility
rules. No market will be selected solely because of a high composite score.

### 6. Investment-Analysis Scope

Where evidence permits, the analysis will cover:

- TAM, SAM, and SOM
- Market and competitor analysis
- Strategic fit and entry options
- Revenue and cost assumptions
- Initial investment and working capital
- Projected cash flows
- Break-even analysis
- Net Present Value (NPV)
- Internal Rate of Return (IRR)
- Downside, base, and upside scenarios
- Sensitivity analysis
- Commercial, regulatory, operational, and financial risks

Methods will be selected according to their relevance and the quality of
available evidence.

### 7. Evidence Standards

Material market claims must be traceable to a source, a documented
assumption, or a reproducible calculation.

Research must distinguish between:

- Historical observations
- Current conditions
- Forecasts and projections
- Aurevia-specific assumptions
- Analytical estimates

Source dates, definitions, geographic coverage, and limitations will be
recorded. Unsupported precision and invented market statistics are
unacceptable.

### 8. Financial-Model Standards

Financial projections must expose their assumptions and calculation logic.

The analysis must distinguish market size from Aurevia's obtainable revenue
and revenue from profit and cash flow.

Investment conclusions must consider downside risk, not just the base case.

NPV, IRR, break-even, and other financial metrics will be reported only
when the model supports meaningful interpretation.

### 9. Permitted Decision Outcomes

- ENTER
- DELAY
- DO NOT ENTER

A recommendation must explain the evidence, assumptions, uncertainties,
trade-offs, and conditions supporting the selected outcome.

### 10. Technical Approach

The project will use public and appropriately sourced data, Python,
pandas, SQL, and PostgreSQL where useful, supported by reproducible
analysis, validation checks, and version control.

Visualisations and advanced analytical methods will be used only where
they improve understanding or decision quality.

AI-assisted work will be documented, material claims will be verified,
and final judgements will remain subject to human review.

### 11. Executive Deliverables

- Board Investment Memo
- Market Attractiveness Assessment
- Entry Economics Model
- Risk and Scenario Analysis
- Source-Traceable Research Appendix

### 12. Quality Gate

The project is complete only when:

- The market universe and screening rules are documented.
- Material evidence is traceable.
- Data transformations and calculations are reproducible.
- Financial assumptions and limitations are explicit.
- Investment conclusions are tested under alternative scenarios.
- Risks and trade-offs are clearly explained.
- The final recommendation follows from the evidence.
- The executive memo communicates the decision and its rationale clearly.
