# Project 2 — Market Entry Strategy
## Market Universe and Eligibility Specification

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how Aurevia Group's candidate-market universe will
be established before market attractiveness is assessed.

Its purpose is to prevent premature market selection, unsupported
assumptions, and arbitrary scoring from influencing the investment decision.

It establishes:

- The principles governing candidate-market selection
- The initial candidate-market universe
- Mandatory eligibility conditions
- Evidence requirements
- Exclusion and conditional-review rules
- The process for moving from the candidate universe to market screening

This specification supports the Project 2 Decision Charter.

---

## 2. Decision Context

Aurevia Group is considering potential international expansion across its
consumer and retail business.

The company operates across four divisions:

- Personal Care
- Home & Lifestyle
- Wellness
- Everyday Essentials

Its commercial channels include:

- Retail
- E-commerce
- Distributors
- Direct-to-Consumer
- Specialty

A country's overall economic size or growth rate does not, by itself,
establish that it is an attractive market for Aurevia.

The assessment must consider the relationship between market demand,
Aurevia's products, competitive conditions, route-to-market feasibility,
investment requirements, and risk.

The ultimate objective is not to identify the most attractive country
in general. It is to determine whether a particular market offers a
defensible opportunity for Aurevia.

---

## 3. Market Selection Principles

The candidate-market process will follow these principles.

### 3.1 Evidence before preference

Markets will not be selected simply because they are familiar, prominent,
fast-growing, or frequently discussed in business media.

Initial candidates are hypotheses to investigate, not preferred outcomes.

### 3.2 Eligibility before attractiveness

Eligibility determines whether a market can reasonably proceed into
structured screening.

Attractiveness determines how eligible markets compare.

These are separate analytical stages.

### 3.3 Product relevance matters

National GDP, population, and consumer spending are contextual indicators.
They are not substitutes for evidence of demand in relevant product
categories.

The analysis will investigate which Aurevia divisions and products could
plausibly serve each market.

### 3.4 Commercial access matters

A market may have significant consumer demand but still be difficult or
uneconomic to serve because of regulation, distribution constraints,
import requirements, localization, or other barriers.

Market size must therefore be considered alongside practical accessibility.

### 3.5 Evidence quality matters

The availability, credibility, comparability, and recency of evidence
will influence whether a market can be assessed reliably.

Missing data will be documented rather than silently replaced with
unsupported estimates.

### 3.6 No predetermined winner

No candidate market will be declared the winner before screening and
subsequent investment analysis are complete.

The final recommendation may be to enter, delay entry, or not enter.

### 3.7 No arbitrary exclusion thresholds

Numerical cut-offs will not be invented merely to make screening appear
more rigorous.

Where quantitative thresholds are later needed, their business rationale,
data requirements, and limitations must be documented.

## 5. Mandatory Eligibility Conditions

Each candidate will be assessed against the following conditions.

### E1. Relevant Consumer Opportunity

There must be a plausible reason to investigate demand for at least one
Aurevia division or product category.

The initial review should consider:

- Evidence of relevant category demand or consumption
- Relevant consumer groups and purchasing power
- Market size or another credible indicator of commercial opportunity
- Historical direction of category or related-market development, where
  reliable evidence is available

A large population alone does not satisfy this condition.

**Decision:** Pass, Conditional, Fail, or Not Assessed.

### E2. Plausible Route to Market

There must be at least one credible route through which Aurevia could
potentially reach customers.

Potential routes include:

- Local distributors
- Retail partners
- E-commerce marketplaces
- Direct-to-consumer channels
- Strategic partnerships
- A locally established operating presence

At this stage, the existence and feasibility of a route to market may
remain uncertain. The objective is to establish whether a plausible
route merits further investigation.

**Decision:** Pass, Conditional, Fail, or Not Assessed.

### E3. Preliminary Regulatory and Legal Feasibility

There must be no established, insurmountable barrier that makes the
proposed business activity clearly infeasible.

The initial review should identify potential requirements relating to:

- Product registration or approval
- Product standards and certification
- Ingredient restrictions, where relevant
- Packaging and labelling
- Import requirements and tariffs
- Foreign ownership or investment restrictions, where relevant
- Other material product-specific or market-specific requirements

Requirements may differ by division and product.

A preliminary review is not a substitute for formal legal or regulatory
due diligence.

**Decision:** Pass, Conditional, Fail, or Not Assessed.

### E4. Preliminary Operating Feasibility

There must be a plausible way to supply and support the market.

The review should consider:

- Logistics and distribution infrastructure
- Import and customs processes
- Potential partner availability
- Product localization requirements
- Supply continuity
- Customer service and after-sales requirements, where applicable

A material uncertainty does not automatically disqualify a market.
It must be documented and assessed.

**Decision:** Pass, Conditional, Fail, or Not Assessed.

### E5. Sufficient Evidence for Structured Assessment

There must be a reasonable prospect of obtaining credible evidence to
assess the market.

At a minimum, the initial evidence review should seek information on:

- Market opportunity and relevant consumer categories
- Population, income, or other relevant consumer indicators
- Economic conditions
- Competitive conditions
- Regulatory and market-access considerations

The review should prioritize authoritative statistics, government and
regulatory sources, credible industry research, and company disclosures.

A lack of directly comparable category data must be recorded as an
evidence limitation. It must not be concealed by combining incompatible
estimates.

**Decision:** Pass, Conditional, Fail, or Not Assessed.

### E6. Strategic Relevance to Aurevia

There must be a plausible strategic rationale for investigating the market.

Potential rationales include:

- Demand for one or more Aurevia product categories
- Potential for expansion through existing capabilities
- Geographic or channel opportunities
- Potential diversification of revenue
- Opportunities to develop capabilities useful in other markets

These are hypotheses to test, not assumed benefits.

A candidate should not pass merely because it offers geographic
diversification or is a large economy.

**Decision:** Pass, Conditional, Fail, or Not Assessed.

## 6. Eligibility Decision Rules

Each mandatory condition will receive one of four statuses.

### PASS

Available evidence supports the conclusion that the condition is
sufficiently satisfied for preliminary screening.

### CONDITIONAL

The market may be suitable for further consideration, but an important
uncertainty or information gap must be resolved.

The condition, evidence gap, and required follow-up must be documented.

### FAIL

Credible evidence indicates that a mandatory condition cannot reasonably
be satisfied within the project's stated business assumptions.

The specific evidence and rationale must be recorded.

### NOT ASSESSED

The available work has not yet established whether the condition is
satisfied.

This is a temporary status, not evidence of failure.

### Overall eligibility

A market may proceed to structured screening when:

- No mandatory condition has been assessed as Fail.
- Material uncertainties are explicitly recorded.
- Evidence is sufficient to support a meaningful preliminary comparison.

A Conditional status does not automatically exclude a market.

If a material regulatory, operating, or evidence issue remains unresolved,
the market may proceed only with an explicit qualification or may be held
pending further investigation.

A market assessed as Fail will not proceed under the current assumptions
unless new evidence or a documented change in assumptions justifies
reconsideration.

No market will be excluded solely because one economic indicator is weak.

## 7. Evidence Requirements

Every material eligibility assessment must be traceable to evidence.

The source register should capture:

- Source ID
- Country
- Topic or eligibility condition
- Claim or indicator being assessed
- Source organization
- Document or dataset title
- Publication date
- Observation period
- Geographic coverage
- Metric definition and unit
- Source location
- Date accessed
- Evidence quality assessment
- Limitations or caveats
- Resulting interpretation
- Eligibility decision supported

### Evidence hierarchy

Preferred sources include:

1. National statistical agencies, central banks, government departments,
   regulators, and official statistical publications.
2. International organizations and authoritative public datasets.
3. Credible industry research, specialist market studies, and reputable
   financial institutions.
4. Established financial press, trade publications, and industry
   associations.

Search snippets, unsourced commentary, and aggregators may help identify
research leads but should not independently support material investment
claims.

### Historical and forward-looking evidence

The research should distinguish between:

- Historical observations
- Current conditions
- Published forecasts
- Aurevia-specific assumptions
- Analytical estimates

Where available, the analysis should examine recent multi-year trends
rather than relying on a single observation.

The date and period of every important statistic must be recorded.

Different measures must not be treated as directly comparable unless
their definitions, coverage, and units support comparison.

---

## 8. Treatment of Missing or Conflicting Evidence

Missing evidence will not automatically be interpreted as poor market
potential.

Likewise, the absence of evidence of a problem will not be treated as
proof that no problem exists.

Where sources conflict:

1. Check whether they measure the same concept.
2. Compare observation periods and geographic coverage.
3. Review methodology and source authority.
4. Document the discrepancy.
5. Prefer the source best suited to the specific question.
6. Preserve material uncertainty where the discrepancy cannot be resolved.

Where estimates are necessary, their methodology and assumptions must
be documented separately from observed data.

Unsupported precision is unacceptable.

---

## 9. Exclusion and Reconsideration Rules

A candidate may be excluded from the current assessment when credible
evidence establishes one or more of the following:

- No plausible demand for any relevant Aurevia product category.
- A material and effectively insurmountable barrier to market access.
- No plausible route to serve customers under the current business
  assumptions.
- A fundamental mismatch between the opportunity and Aurevia's
  capabilities that cannot reasonably be addressed.
- Insufficient evidence to support a meaningful assessment after
  reasonable research efforts.

These rules must be applied consistently.

Exclusion decisions must identify the failed condition, supporting
evidence, assumptions, and date of decision.

A candidate may be reconsidered if circumstances or assumptions change.

---

## 10. Eligibility Is Not a Market Ranking

Passing eligibility does not establish that a market is attractive.

Eligible markets will proceed to a separate screening stage covering:

- Market opportunity
- Economic environment
- Consumer potential
- Competitive environment
- Strategic fit
- Entry feasibility and risk

The screening methodology will specify relevant indicators, data
definitions, direction of preference, evidence quality, and treatment
of missing values before comparative results are interpreted.

If a composite score is ultimately used, its design and limitations must
be documented. It will be a decision aid, not an automatic investment
recommendation.

A high screening score cannot substitute for product-level analysis,
entry economics, or risk assessment.

---

## 11. Expected Outputs

This stage must produce:

1. A documented candidate-market universe.
2. A completed eligibility assessment for each candidate.
3. An evidence register supporting material assessments.
4. A record of exclusions, conditional decisions, and unresolved gaps.
5. A rationale for any additions or removals from the candidate universe.
6. A list of markets eligible to proceed to structured screening.

The eligibility assessment should be reproducible and open to review.

---

## 12. Quality Gate

The market-universe stage is complete only when:

- The candidate universe is documented.
- Eligibility conditions are defined before comparative ranking.
- Every candidate has an assessment against every mandatory condition.
- Material conclusions are supported by traceable evidence.
- Missing information is distinguished from negative evidence.
- Conditional decisions have documented follow-up requirements.
- Exclusions have explicit, evidence-based rationales.
- No preferred market has been assumed in advance.
- The eligible candidate set is justified before market scoring begins.

---

## 13. Governing Principle

The purpose of market eligibility is to determine which opportunities
deserve structured investigation.

The purpose of market screening is to compare those opportunities.

The purpose of investment analysis is to determine whether a specific
entry decision creates sufficient value for Aurevia.

These are separate decisions and must not be collapsed into a single
country ranking.
